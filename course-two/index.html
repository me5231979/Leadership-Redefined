    Module 4: Communication, Storytelling & Brand · Brand and reputation
    1

    Brand and reputation: close the gap.

    Brand is what you say about yourself. Reputation is what others say about you. They are separate but related. The goal is to close the gap in order to achieve successful business results.

    Module 4: Communication, Storytelling & Brand · Where are we today?

    Where Vanderbilt stands today.

    Ertel asked: where are we today? He named three challenges.

    1 · Limited brand saliency

    Vanderbilt currently has strong brand awareness in the Southern region but struggles to gain the same level of recognition nationally.

    2 · Lack of differentiation

    For many, Vanderbilt lacks distinction compared to peer institutions. This perceived lack of differentiation reduces the university’s ability to effectively convert awareness to consideration.

    3 · Universities under fire

    Vanderbilt, like all universities, must navigate the devaluation of the higher education industry, driven by issues around affordability, the admissions process, political ideologies and increasing polarization.

    From Steve Ertel’s Brand and Reputation presentation, Leadership Redefined Symposium, October 2026.

    Module 4: Communication, Storytelling & Brand · Three flywheels

    Differentiation by design.

    Vanderbilt is a fighter brand. Not the loudest. Deeply valued once discovered. High affinity, low saliency.

    Differentiation as a design principle
    1. Three reputation flywheels set Vanderbilt apart: execution with ambition, values-driven leadership, and athletics momentum.
    2. Each flywheel shows two kinds of assets: a brand asset, what Vanderbilt says, and a reputation asset, what others say.

    Module 5: Entrepreneurial Mindset · Radical Collaboration

    Radical Collaboration, in three principles.

    The Chancellor’s call for Radical Collaboration underpinned our key fundraising principles. The objective is to maximize total philanthropic support for Vanderbilt to advance its mission.

    We are always donor-centric
    • Our gift proposals will reflect donor interests and passions in alignment with the university’s goals and mission
    • University priorities are best served by working with donors, rather than trying to steer them in a particular direction
    Fundraising is a team sport
    • Multiple roles are needed to execute our strategies
    • Every Vanderbilt leader is a fundraiser
    We operate as One Vanderbilt
    • Donors who support multiple areas give the most
    • All key prospects have cross-Vanderbilt strategies supported by “shared credit” mechanisms

    From Vice Chancellor John Lutz’s presentation, DAR Transformational Change, October 2026.

    Module 5: Entrepreneurial Mindset · Four categories

    Four categories of change.

    We thoughtfully constructed four categories of change initiatives, and developed a rigorous system to manage them.

    1. 1People and Culture
    2. 2Collaboration and Engagement
    3. 3Motivation and Incentives
    4. 4Processes and Systems

    “Think outside the constraints of Higher Education!”

    See the initiatives on his slides
    • People and culture, where the transformation started: develop core values; expand leadership; improve communications; invest in professional development; attract talent.
    • Collaboration and engagement, crucial both intramurally and extramurally: rebuild relationships with deans and other campus partners; create infrastructure to encourage collaboration on cross-institutional gifts; drive more intention to donor centricity.
    • Motivation and incentives, in his Retaining Key Talent section: rigorous goal setting process; balanced performance indicators for gift officers; individual goal achievement and team recognition at a balance point.
    • Processes and systems, crucial to change drivers: implement new CRM; create kaizen culture; introduce data analytics; enhance quality and completeness of data; simplify and enhance processes; operationalize the “sales” function.
