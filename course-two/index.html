    Module 4: Communication, Storytelling & Brand · Story and brand
    1

    Start with the Chancellor on communication.

    Brand is what you say about yourself. Reputation is what others say about you. The goal is to close the gap.

    The idea
    1. Separate but related. Closing the gap is how brand work delivers results.
    2. Vanderbilt’s candid read: known in the South, less known nationally, seen as similar to peers.
    3. By design, not by default. Tell your own story, or the world tells it.

    The brand work: 2022 identity, 2023 state of the brand, 2024 strategy, 2025 activation.

    Module 4: Communication, Storytelling & Brand · The ground game

    A spike without a system is noise.

    Vanderbilt is a fighter brand: not the loudest, but deeply valued once discovered.

    The idea
    1. The ladder. Relevance, then consideration, then trust, then commitment.
    2. Spike and drumbeat. A spike moment lasts only on top of a steady drumbeat.
    3. You are the ground game. Carry stories to priority people. Trust travels through proximity.

    How this story ends.

    Years of mediocre performance, then records. Guess first.

    Tap a tile, make your guess

    What drove it

    The team named root causes, then changed people, process, and technology together. On three-year rolling averages, 2020 to 2025, new commitments grew from $149M to $314M while the investment grew from $28M to $44M. Results grew faster than spending, and the team kept an eye on efficiency.

    Early wins built momentum: events and hospitality, CRM and data, bringing the campaign to life, additional funding.

    Module 5: Entrepreneurial Mindset · The change moves

    Change people, process, and technology at once.

    Successful change leadership requires ambidexterity: alternating between assessment and action.

    The idea
    1. Assess: listen carefully to key constituents; make clear-eyed assessments.
    2. Act: deal with resistance; drive people, process, and technology together.
    3. Look beyond best practices for new ideas, even outside higher education.

    Four categories held every initiative: people and culture, collaboration and engagement, motivation and incentives, process and systems.

    Module 5: Entrepreneurial Mindset · The initiatives

    Module 5: Entrepreneurial Mindset · Your pilot

    Reframe it, then start small.

    Question the status quo, take a calculated risk, and let an early win build momentum.

    The idea
    1. Question the assumption. Flip it with “what if.”
    2. Look beyond best practices, including outside higher education.
    3. Pilot, validate, then scale. Agree first on the result that earns scale.

    Example. A past pod asked: what if attending campus events seeded a student’s investment account? Their plan piloted first, then went campus-wide.

    Your brief · Strong metrics

    Would you know in 90 days if it worked?

    A strong metric names what changes, by how much, for whom, and by when.

    Anatomy of a metric
    1. Baseline: where it is today. Without it, no one can tell if you improved.
    2. Target: the number you are aiming for.
    3. Date: when you will check.
    4. Outcome, not output: measure what changes for people, like days saved or students returning, not what you produced, like emails sent or a site launched.
