1/8Which four categories organized the fundraising turnaround’s change initiatives?

2/8Your pod’s challenge involves human behavior, many causes, and no known answer. What kind of problem is it, and what is the right move?

Complex problems call for probe, sense, respond (Snowden and Boone, 2007). Hence pilot, learn, scale.

3/8What does “look beyond best practices” mean in Lutz’s case?

Think outside the constraints of higher education.

4/8In the reputation feedback loop, what does reputation produce?

Reputation attracts resources, and resources build reputation. The loop can run in reverse too.

5/8A mistake from your office is spreading online. Which response protects trust best?

Transparency, expertise, commitment, and empathy together. Speed and honesty shrink a mistake.

6/8“You can’t always control for winning, but you can always control your ability to compete.” What does it ask of a team leader?

Control the controllables. Hackman’s research points the same way: leaders shape the conditions.

7/8High standards with low psychological safety put a team in which zone?

Anxiety. People work hard but hide problems. Add safety and you reach the learning zone (Edmondson, 2019).

8/8Which is a strong success metric for a capstone brief?

Baseline, target, date, and an outcome people feel.

You are here: consolidation. Summary

What you now know.

Diagnose before you build

Lutz’s turnaround started with benchmarking and a blunt SWOT, then moved through ambidexterity: alternating assessment and action.

At work: compare yourself to the best, not the average.

Change three things at once

People and Culture, Collaboration and Engagement, Motivation and Incentives, Process and Systems, each with owners and dates.

At work: a new tool alone fixes nothing.

Probe complex problems

When there is no known answer, run a small pilot, learn, and scale what works.

At work: propose the pilot before the rollout.

Reputation is infrastructure

It attracts talent, funding, partnerships, and access, and it rests on trust: transparency, expertise, commitment, and empathy.

At work: every promise kept is a deposit.

Control the controllables

Candice Storey Lee builds teams on values, preparation, patience with good people, and growth with accountability.

At work: celebrate the win, then point at the next one.

Aim for the learning zone

High standards plus psychological safety. Teams do best with dependability, clarity, meaning, and impact too.

At work: ask for concerns, and thank people who raise them.

Glossary

Ambidexterity:
alternating between assessment and action while leading change.
Benchmarking:
comparing your results with peers, ideally the best ones.
CAGR:
compound annual growth rate; the average yearly growth over a period.
Complex problem:
one with no known answer up front, where cause and effect show up only in hindsight.
CRM:
customer relationship management system; software that tracks relationships, such as with donors.
Dependability:
team members trusting each other to deliver quality work on time.
Kaizen:
a practice of small, continuous improvements, borrowed from manufacturing.
Learning zone:
high standards together with high psychological safety.
Pilot:
a small, time-limited test of an idea before a full rollout.
Psychological safety:
a shared belief that it is safe to speak up, ask, and admit mistakes.
Reframe:
stating a problem in a new way that opens different solutions.
Reputation feedback loop:
reputation attracts resources, and resources build reputation.
Reputational statement:
one or two sentences on why a project matters for Vanderbilt’s credibility and visibility.
Revenue sharing:
the college sports model under which schools share revenue directly with student-athletes.
SWOT:
strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats.
Team charter:
a short agreement on purpose, owners, decisions, and accountability.
