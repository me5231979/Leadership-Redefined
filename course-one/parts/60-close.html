1/8Which sentence is Vanderbilt’s institutional vision?

Define it and be it. The industry leadership vision adds: help reclaim the essential role of leading research universities in driving American prosperity.

2/8How does the strategy say Vanderbilt will achieve the vision?

Uncommon speed, agility and scale, applied to relevant societal challenges through education and research.

3/8Using the value stick, which work counts as strategic?

Value created is the gap between the two. If no stakeholder’s choice changes, it is activity, not strategy (Oberholzer-Gee, 2021).

4/8What is the difference between brand and reputation?

Separate but related. The goal is to close the gap between them.

5/8“A spike without a system is noise.” What does that mean for a leader?

Spike plus drumbeat. Reputation is infrastructure, a system rather than a campaign, and the ground game carries it to people who matter.

6/8In Duarte’s structure, how does a persuasive story move?

The contrast creates tension. End with the call to action and the new bliss.

7/8In the Chancellor’s framing, mission is purpose. What is margin?

Margin is capacity, and building it is everybody’s job. Financial discipline enables strategic investments.

8/8An activity has high mission impact but costs more than it brings in. On the matrix it is a…

A Heart. Healthy only when something else, often a Star or a Money Tree, is named as its funding.

You are here: consolidation. Summary

What you now know.

The vision is an instruction

Define the great university of the 21st century and be it, at uncommon speed, agility and scale, through three focus areas.

At work: translate it into your team’s terms; no one else will.

Strategy is a choice

Strategic work raises willingness to pay or lowers willingness to sell. Everything else is activity.

At work: ask whose choice changes before you start.

Momentum is measurable

Media mentions, Early Decision applications, and new commitments all rose sharply, driven by three reputation flywheels.

At work: know which flywheel your team turns.

Close the gap

Brand is what you say; reputation is what others say. A fighter brand earns attention, interrupts expectation, designs bold moments, and sustains meaning.

At work: you are the ground game; trust travels through proximity.

Stories move people

Move between what is and what could be, open with a real person, and end with a clear call to action.

At work: name the audience before you write a word.

Mission needs margin

Mission is purpose, margin is capacity. Discipline in good years buys choices in hard ones.

At work: every proposal names its mission benefit and its cost.

Glossary

Air cover:
broad campaigns and national stories that create reputation assets.
Brand:
what an organization says about itself.
Drumbeat:
a steady flow of stories between big moments that keeps trust building.
Fighter brand:
not the loudest, deeply valued once discovered; high affinity, low saliency.
Focus area:
one of the three ways Vanderbilt delivers on the vision, and the category your capstone is judged in.
Ground game:
sharing reputation assets directly with priority stakeholders, who pass them on peer to peer.
Heart, Star, Money Tree, Stop Sign:
the four quadrants of the mission and margin matrix.
Margin:
capacity; the financial strength to pursue the mission.
Mission:
purpose; research, teaching, and discovery.
New commitments:
philanthropic gifts and pledges made in a year, counted when promised rather than when paid.
Pod:
your capstone team.
Reputation:
what others say about an organization.
Saliency:
how readily a brand comes to mind.
Spike moment:
a burst of attention, such as an upset win or a major gift.
Value stick:
Oberholzer-Gee’s tool; value created is the gap between willingness to pay and willingness to sell.
Willingness to pay:
the most a student, family, donor, or partner would give for what you offer.
Willingness to sell:
the least an employee or supplier would accept to work with you.
