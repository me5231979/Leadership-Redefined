Module A

Why it matters now

“Universities face pressure: rising costs, declining support, higher expectations. Leaders must connect values and viability.” That is how Chancellor Diermeier framed this topic for the program.

You’ll learnThe core idea, and what peers are doing Time4 min

Select a side of the cycle

Each one feeds the other. Neither is the point on its own.

Mission = Purpose

Research, teaching, and discovery. Mission is why Vanderbilt exists, and it guides which investments are worth making.

For a leader: use mission to set priorities, so the budget conversation starts with “what matters most” instead of “what did we spend last year.”

Margin = Capacity

The financial strength to pursue the mission. Margin is what lets Vanderbilt make bold investments when peers are cutting.

For a leader: “Financial discipline enables strategic investments,” and “building financial capacity is everybody’s job,” in the Chancellor’s words.

What peers did in 2025

The Chancellor showed the program headlines from peer campuses, among them: Northwestern eliminating more than 400 staff positions amid “mounting financial pressures”; Columbia laying off nearly 180 researchers funded by federal grants; Brown extending a staff hiring freeze; and hiring and raise pauses at Johns Hopkins after federal cuts. A side-by-side of about 25 research universities tracked layoffs, hiring freezes, salary freezes, budget cuts, capital freezes, and graduate program cuts.

Vanderbilt’s column on that chart had none of those actions marked. That is the payoff of margin: discipline in good years buys choices in hard ones.

Source: Chancellor Daniel Diermeier, “Mission and Margin,” presentation to Leadership Redefined, 2026, citing the Daily Northwestern, Columbia Spectator, Brown Daily Herald, and Forbes.

Module B

The mindset and the levers

Four habits of mind, four practical levers. Flip each mindset card for what it looks like on a real Tuesday, then make two calls.

You’ll doFlip four cards, make two calls Time6 min
The four key levers

Understand where money comes from and where it goes. Use data and discipline to inform choices. Collaborate: academic and finance partnership. Pilot, learn, scale. The last lever is the bridge to next week’s entrepreneurial mindset: small bets protect margin while they test the mission case.

Scenario 01

The beloved program

A small program serves 40 students a year with real impact. It costs three times what similar programs cost per student, and your budget is flat next year.

Our take: high-mission, low-margin work is worth keeping when you are honest about what pays for it. Separate the outcome you must protect from the way you currently deliver it.

Lever: data and discipline
Scenario 02

The revenue idea

A partner offers to pay well to rent your unit’s space and staff time for an outside program. It would fund two new positions, but it is unrelated to your mission.

Our take: off-mission revenue can be healthy if you cap the staff time it takes and decide in advance which mission work it funds. Write that down before you sign.

Lever: where money comes from and goes
Module C

Balance a portfolio on the matrix

You lead a unit with five activities and 100 points of discretionary investment. Spread the points, and watch where your portfolio lands on the mission and margin matrix. There is no single right answer; the read tells you what your choice implies.

You’ll learnHow to see trade-offs at a glance You’ll doAllocate 100 points Time6 min
The idea

The matrix plots every activity on two axes: mission impact (up) and financial contribution (right). Stars are high on both: invest. Hearts are high-mission but cost money: keep them, and know what pays for them. Money Trees make money with little mission tie: harvest them on purpose. Stop Signs are low on both: stop, or give them away.
