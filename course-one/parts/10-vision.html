The idea

The vision has two halves. The institutional vision: Vanderbilt will define the great university of the 21st century and be it. The industry leadership vision: Vanderbilt will help reclaim the essential role of leading research universities in driving American prosperity. The way there is to operate at uncommon speed, agility and scale. Read those three phrases as instructions to every leader: move faster than peers, adapt when conditions change, and build things that reach more people.

Execution with Ambition

Doing big things, and doing them well: record fundraising, new campuses in New York, San Francisco, and West Palm Beach, and quantum work in Chattanooga.

Your part: the projects your team ships on time, at quality, at a scale that surprises people. Exceptional core operations are the fuel.

Values-Driven Leadership

Being known for what Vanderbilt stands for: open forums, institutional neutrality, and civil discourse. These are the shared values that give the university clarity of purpose.

Your part: how you handle disagreement, fairness, and hard calls when no one outside the room is watching.

Athletics Momentum

When Vanderbilt beat No. 1 Alabama in October 2024, millions of people who never think about a university suddenly thought about this one. Athletics converts attention to affinity faster than almost anything else.

Your part: you will meet the leader behind that momentum, Vice Chancellor Candice Storey Lee, in Course Two’s section on high-performing teams.

Module C

Strategy is a choice: the value stick

The program’s pre-work, Felix Oberholzer-Gee’s Better, Simpler Strategy, gives every leader one test for whether work is strategic. Learn the tool, then sort six real-sounding initiatives.

You’ll learnThe one question that separates strategy from activity You’ll doSort six initiatives Time6 min
The idea

Picture a stick. At the top is willingness to pay: the most a student, family, donor, or partner would give for what Vanderbilt offers. At the bottom is willingness to sell: the least an employee or supplier would accept to work with Vanderbilt. The space between them is the value Vanderbilt creates. Oberholzer-Gee’s point is simple: strategic work either raises willingness to pay or lowers willingness to sell. Everything else is activity.

In a university, “pay” is rarely only money. It is a student choosing Vanderbilt over a peer, a donor giving more, a researcher bringing a grant. “Sell” is a great colleague choosing to stay because the work is meaningful and the systems do not fight them.

1/6Admitted students get a decision on financial aid in three days instead of three weeks.

2/6Staff can finish a purchase request in one system instead of three.

3/6A new monthly report that no one has asked for and no decision depends on.

4/6Every major donor gets one coordinated Vanderbilt relationship instead of separate asks from five schools.

5/6A clear career path and development program for early-career staff in your division.

6/6Rebranding a committee with a new name and logo, with the same members and charge.

Module D

Find your line of sight

Strategy only becomes real when a leader can draw a straight line from their own work to the vision. Three quick scenarios; choose what you would do, then read our take.

You’ll doMake three calls Time5 min
Scenario 01

The pet project

Your team has run the same annual event for eight years. It takes six weeks of staff time. Attendance is flat and no one can say what it changes.

Our take: test it before you defend it or kill it. If a stakeholder’s choice moves because of it, improve it. If not, give the six weeks to something that does.

Focus: Exceptional Core Operations
Scenario 02

The question from your team

A team member asks, “What does ‘the great university of the 21st century’ have to do with processing reimbursements?”

Our take: uncommon speed, agility and scale is built from thousands of ordinary transactions done well. Translating the vision into your team’s terms is the leader’s job, and no one else will do it for you.

Focus: Exceptional Core Operations
Scenario 03

The bold idea with no home

A colleague in another school has an idea that would help both of your units, but neither budget owns it and it would mean sharing credit.

Our take: “shared credit” mechanisms were one of the changes that helped Vanderbilt’s fundraising turnaround. Make the pilot small enough to start without permission and visible enough to scale.
